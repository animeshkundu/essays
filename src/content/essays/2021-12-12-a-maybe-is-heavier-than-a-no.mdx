A couple in Grand Rapids planned a trip to Mexico for their fiftieth wedding anniversary, lost it to the 2020 surge, and rebuilt it for this December with their two kids and their families: snorkeling, zip lines, rivers that run through caves, everything a Grand Rapids December is not. "We're ready," the wife told a reporter. "We're all ready." Then the new variant was named, and now she will only say she is "waiting and watching," "not ready yet to cancel or postpone," unwilling to put "everybody at risk"; the trip is still on the calendar, and it can no longer be trusted. The variant barely had to move: no one yet knows how sick it makes people, the airlines had just posted their busiest week since the pandemic began, and domestic online flight bookings rose nineteen percent against the week before. The real weight of a virus lands on bodies, not calendars, and deaths were already climbing past a thousand a day from the wave already here, so beside that a postponed vacation should weigh nothing. But a society can carry bad news for a long time; what wears it thin is the hope that will not resolve. A no you grieve, refund, and replace, but a maybe asks a couple who have already waited a year to stay ready and unready at once, for weeks, with no door to walk through. Somewhere on your own calendar is a plan you have quietly stopped believing in.
