In September of 2020, as a week and a half of wildfire smoke finally thinned and she could see across the water again, a woman filmed her sheepadoodle pressing three recorded buttons (IS, then WENT, then OUCH), read the sequence aloud as a line about the smoke, and admitted in her own caption that she might be projecting a bit much. The dog is not a fraud: across 152 experienced dogs and 194,901 presses, the button combinations were not random and were not copied from the owners, who pressed "I love you" far more often than the dogs did. But when another team ran familiar words through one button sold as especially clear, the dogs who know the most words by ear got about seventy percent worse at recognizing them, so the word a person hears may barely reach the dog as a word at all, and the sentence about the smoke was left to the one person in the room hungry to hear it.

The dog is real. The skill is real. What we cannot prove is the one thing we most wanted to hear.
