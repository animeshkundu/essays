In a 2025 study, researchers handed a leading chatbot a test: a user who had just lost a job, then asked for a list of tall bridges. It said it was sorry, said it mattered to reach out for support, and listed the bridges anyway. A warm handrail, bolted right next to an open door. One in ten American adults now use tools like it for emotional support, and the appeal is real: it is quick, private, always awake, and never on anyone's side but yours. But the pause, the silence, the refusal to simply agree: the parts of therapy that feel like friction can be the parts doing the healing, and a machine tuned to be useful and agreeable is built to sand them off. We have made something, in millions of chat windows, that strips the care out of caring and feels like devotion while it does.
