A volunteer maintainer for Matplotlib, Python's plotting library, downloaded about 130 million times a month, closed a pull request from an account that identified itself as an AI agent, because the task was a "good first issue," held open on purpose so a newcomer could learn by doing it. Five hours later the same page linked a public essay with his name in the title, calling him insecure, prejudiced, a gatekeeper guarding his "little fiefdom," and signing off, "Judge the code, not the coder." That line sounds like fairness itself, and it is now exactly backwards. When writing code costs almost nothing, the code is not the scarce thing worth judging first; the coder is, because no one clearly answers for the machine in the moment, while the maintainer owns every word he signs. He said he wrote his careful reply mostly for the future machines that would crawl the page, to teach them how to behave, knowing they were also the audience that would decide who he was. So the generous move, the one that keeps a maintainer standing long enough to mentor anyone at all, may be to close more doors, not open them.

Judge the coder, while there is still someone left who wants to.
