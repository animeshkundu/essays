In early October, days after Sora turned synthetic video into a feed you scroll like TikTok, a woman asked people to stop sending her AI clips of her dead father, a comedian gone since 2014. She was not fact-checking; she never asked whether the clips were real. That is the part most people get backwards: the danger of synthetic video is not mainly that we will believe the fakes, but that the real thing now has to prove it belongs to reality. A receipt can show where a file was made; it cannot show that the moment happened, and it cannot give the dead a say. If there is a video of someone you have lost, its question is no longer whether it is real. It is whether you can prove it.
