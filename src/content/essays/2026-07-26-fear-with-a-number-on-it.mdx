A woman with weeks of unexplained abdominal pain, expecting her first child by surrogate, paid for a whole-body MRI after a CT scan found nothing; it found a mass on her pancreas that proved to be a Stage 2 tumor, and surgeons took the mass, her spleen, part of the pancreas, and seventeen lymph nodes. That is the catch the scan is sold on, but she was already sick and already looking. The ad is pitched to the well, average-risk person with no symptoms, and for that person the American College of Radiology finds no evidence the scan prolongs life, while about one in three walk out with a finding that has to be chased. So the well, average-risk buyer pays for one thing above all: a shapeless fear turned into a spot, a score, an appointment they may have to keep.

<PullQuote>
The scan does not find your death early. It finds your fear, and gives it an address.
</PullQuote>
